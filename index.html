What is People Over Papers?

People Over Papers is a grassroots, community-built map where people anonymously report sightings of immigration enforcement activity in their area. It started as a simple shared online board and spread quickly across social media as families, students and workers looked for a way to stay informed about what was happening in their own neighborhoods.

The idea is simple: when one person sees something, everyone nearby can know about it within minutes. Instead of relying on rumors in group chats, the People Over Papers map puts reports in one visual place, pinned to a location with a time and short description. You can view live community reports and recent updates on the People Over Papers map.

What does "People Over Papers" mean?

The phrase puts human dignity ahead of immigration documents. "Papers" refers to legal status paperwork; "people" refers to the neighbors, coworkers and family members behind them. The slogan has been used for years by immigrant-rights groups, and the map adopted it to signal its purpose: community care first.

How the People Over Papers map works

01

Someone sees activity

A community member notices enforcement activity and opens the map on their phone.

02

They submit a pin

They drop a pin with the location, time and a brief note — no account or name required.

03

Neighbors are alerted

Others checking the map see the report and can plan routes or contact rapid response groups.

04

Reports age out

Because situations change fast, older pins lose relevance. Always look at the timestamp.

Important: Reports are crowdsourced. Some pins may be unconfirmed, outdated or mistaken. Treat the map as a starting point and confirm with a trusted local rapid response network.

Want to see how this looks in practice? Open peopleoverpapersmap.net to browse reports by area and check the latest timestamps before you head out.

Safety and privacy tips for using People Over Papers

  • Never post identifying details. Leave out names, faces, license plates, home addresses and photos of individuals.
  • Report what you saw, not what you heard. Second-hand rumors cause panic and reduce trust in the map.
  • Add a clear time. A report without a time is hard for others to judge.
  • Use a private browser window if you share a device, and consider a trusted VPN.
  • Keep your distance. Observing and documenting is legal in many places; physically interfering is not and can put people at risk.
  • Check before you share. Forwarding unverified screenshots can spread false alarms.

Know your rights

A map is only one tool. Knowing your rights matters just as much. Trusted organizations publish free, multilingual guides on what to do if immigration officers approach you at home, at work or on the street:

  • ACLU – Immigrants' Rights
  • National Immigration Law Center (NILC)
  • ILRC – Red Cards (printable rights cards)
  • Immigration Advocates Network – Find free legal help

Many cities also run local rapid response hotlines staffed by trained volunteers. Save your local number in your phone before you need it.

Frequently asked questions

What is People Over Papers?

It is a community-driven, anonymous map where people share sightings of immigration enforcement activity so neighbors can stay informed and safe.

Is People Over Papers anonymous?

Reports are designed to be submitted without an account or personal details. Still, avoid sharing anything that could identify you or anyone else.

Are the reports verified?

Not always. Reports are crowdsourced, so check the timestamp and confirm with a local rapid response group before acting.

Is it legal to use the People Over Papers map?

Sharing publicly observable information is generally protected speech in the U.S., but laws vary. If you are unsure, consult a local legal aid organization.

What should I do if I see enforcement activity?

Stay calm, keep a safe distance, note the time and place, and call your local rapid response hotline. Don't physically interfere.
